Get Active On Github!

I have done my fair share of evaluating code-school grads for junior developer positions. When doing this, Github is often one of my go-to resources. When evaluating a candidate’s Github, my team and I are trying to answer 3 questions:

Did the candidate continue to code and learn after Code School?

Did the candidate tinker and play with technologies and tools outside of Code School?

Did the candidate ever contribute to Open Source by participating in Issue discussion or submitting Pull Requests?

When reviewing a candidate’s Github we were not evaluating technical skill. Instead, my team and I focused on finding individuals that showed a passion for learning and drive to be a part of the Open Source community. Participating in Open Source can take many forms. Everyone can help Open Source projects by triaging issues or updating documentation. It is also great to see continued self-improvement via code-kata practice or work on a personal project.

Contributing For First Timers

Attend Local Meetups and Technical Conferences

Meetups and local hack-nights are an amazing source of knowledge when it comes to knowing what companies in your area are hiring. No one knows more about the industry than the people who work in it. Start attending your local area tech meetups. Seek out individuals working at companies looking to grow and expand their engineering team.

Find A Tech Meetup Near You

There are also larger conferences that can help a great deal towards growing your technical skills and professional network. Some conferences like All Things Open work hard to keep their tickets at an affordable price-point so that anyone can attend and get a career started in technology. They also price tickets for students at $49/day and $79 for both days!

allthingsopen.org/register-now/Joining a local or global technology-focused Slack community can also help a great deal when building your professional network. The people at ThisDot recently published a great blog post containing a hand-curated list of amazing tech-focused Slack communities everyone should check out.

Start Helping Others

Code School graduates I speak with often don’t feel that they are ready to start helping others. However, the people best equipped to help beginners are those who recently were beginners themselves. Many code schools have TA and tutoring programs set up to help new students. Working and volunteering in these programs will build your technical skills and your experience portfolio.

Outside of assisting code schools, there are also events like RailsBridge that are often looking for teachers or teacher-assistants to help with their events. I’d like to specifically shout-out my hometowns own local Triangle RailsBridge event which has been going strong for over 4 years!

In Conclusion

I encourage everyone to follow this three-pronged approach when starting a career in tech. Focus on building your own technical skills, building your professional network and helping others looking to enter the community. Also, reach out to senior individuals in your area and find a mentor that can help put you on the path to success.
