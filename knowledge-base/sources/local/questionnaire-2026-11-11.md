I have been a community meetup organizer for over a decade. I was a frequent guest instructor at the Iron Yard when code schools were at their peak. Back when I worked at Smashing Boxes, I created the Smashing Boxes onboarding program for Code School hires, which was a 90-day intensive ramp-up program where you were assigned a mentor, onboarded onto a project, and taught agile software development, how it was done at the company, and how to be most impactful at your job. I worked hands-on as a mentor during the 3 years that this program ran. During that time, I would work hands-on with engineers on things such as understanding tickets, how to onboard into client code bases, how to understand code someone else wrote, how to better configure your development environment, text editor, terminal, servers, DevOps, and how to deal with other edge case things that you might not have come up with during your typical onboarding. And then also how to collaborate effectively with engineers and how to attend standups routinely and how to be impactful for your team and reliable and dependable so that everyone is supporting each other. I also was the lead of our weekly company-wide standup that involved 50 people where We would give shoutouts to each other and cross-department shoutouts. I was the MC for that every single week, Friday at noon, and conducted that meeting and made sure that everyone was given the appropriate allotted amount of time and made sure that new projects, client releases, and things like that were brought up to the entire team and presented to them by the team that worked on them. So I guess in that way I'm supporting and helping and building up my team. I also worked as a volunteer for programs like Girl Develop It and RailsBridge, which were programs that would take underrepresented people from underrepresented backgrounds in technology and onboard them to the popular coding framework at the time, which was Ruby on Rails. During that time, we would have 50 people in a room, intensive classroom-like experience, over 5 hours where we would walk them through setting up their environment for coding and then how to build essentially a Twitter clone. For RailsBridge, I was a founding member for the Triangle area, and I sat on the board of organizers, and I was also a mentor instructor at all the events.
